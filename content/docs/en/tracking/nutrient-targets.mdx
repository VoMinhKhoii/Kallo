Every card on the [Nutrition](/docs/tracking/nutrition) page compares what you
ate with a daily target. Each target is a published reference intake for your
age and sex. Kallo adjusts one only where the source itself gives a rule for
it: iron follows your weight, and fiber and a few B vitamins follow your
calorie target. Kallo doesn't invent or tune any number.

## Which reference table you get

If Vietnam is your country of origin or residence, your targets come from the
Vietnam RDA 2016, published by the National Institute of Nutrition and the
Ministry of Health. Everyone else gets the WHO/FAO 2004 reference values.
WHO/FAO publishes nothing for copper, manganese, phosphorus, potassium, sodium
or fiber, so those come from the US National Academies (NASEM) for everyone.

Your profile picks the row in that table:

| What your profile has | What the target uses |
| --- | --- |
| Your age | The table's band for that age |
| No age | The band for a 20-year-old |
| No biological sex | The average of the male and female values |
| No weight | The table value, with no weight adjustment |
| No calorie target | The table value, with no calorie adjustment |

How much iron your body absorbs depends on what else you eat, so each table
prints several iron columns. Kallo uses the Vietnam table's 10% column and WHO's 15% column. WHO
gives women after menopause 7.5 mg of iron a day, and Kallo applies it from
age 51, where the US tables also start their older-women band.

## Iron follows your weight

Iron is the one target these tables work out from body weight. The body loses
about 14 µg of iron per kg per day through skin and gut (WHO/FAO 2004; IOM
2001), and for an adult man that loss is the whole requirement. Each table
assumes a reference body weight, so Kallo adds the extra loss for every kg you
weigh above it:

| Table | Adult reference weight, men / women | Extra iron per kg |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vietnam RDA 2016 | 61.1 / 53.0 kg (ages 20–29) | 0.195 mg |
| WHO/FAO 2004 | 75 / 62 kg | 0.121 mg |

For a 22-year-old man:

| Weight | Vietnam | WHO/FAO |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50 kg | 11.9 mg | 9.1 mg |
| 75 kg | 14.6 mg | 9.1 mg |
| 125 kg | 24.3 mg | 15.2 mg |

If you weigh less than the reference, you keep the table value, which already
covers the lighter half of the people it was set for. Each age band has its
own reference weight, and the target never goes above 45 mg a day, the
tolerable upper intake for adults.

For women, period losses don't depend on weight, so only the body-weight part
moves. A 25-year-old Vietnamese woman at 70 kg gets 26.1 + 17 × 0.195 =
29.4 mg.

Kallo uses your latest weigh-in, so logging a new weight updates the target
straight away.

## B vitamins and fiber follow your calorie target

B1 and niacin help the body turn food into energy, so some sources state them
per 1,000 kcal eaten. NASEM states fiber the same way. For these, the target
rises with your calorie target. The table value stays the floor, so a cut
never lowers a target.

| Nutrient | Per 1,000 kcal | Applies to | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vitamin B1 (thiamin) | 0.5 mg | Vietnam table | Vietnam RDA 2016 |
| Vitamin B1 (thiamin) | 0.42 mg | Everyone else | EFSA 2016 |
| Vitamin B2 (riboflavin) | 0.6 mg | Vietnam table | Vietnam RDA 2016 |
| Niacin | 6.7 mg | Everyone | EFSA 2014 |
| Fiber | 14 g | Everyone | NASEM 2005 |

Outside Vietnam B2 stays at the table value: Europe's food safety authority
(EFSA) sets riboflavin at a flat 1.6 mg a day.

On an ordinary intake these targets stay at the table value. A Vietnamese
man's B1 starts rising only above 2,600 kcal. On a 4,375 kcal bulk, a 22-year-old man gets:

| Nutrient | Vietnam | Elsewhere |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vitamin B1 | 2.2 mg | 1.8 mg |
| Vitamin B2 | 2.6 mg | 1.3 mg |
| Niacin | 29 mg | 29 mg |
| Fiber | 61 g | 61 g |

<Callout label="A target is not a diagnosis">
A food log can't tell you whether you are low in a nutrient. The US Institute
of Medicine says intake data alone can't judge one person's nutritional
status; that takes a clinical exam and blood tests. Read these targets as a
reference to compare your log against, and see a doctor if you are worried
about a deficiency.
</Callout>

Kallo has no iodine target: its food data has no iodine figures, and most
iodine comes from iodized salt anyway.
