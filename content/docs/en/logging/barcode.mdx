For packaged food the label already has the numbers. Nothing needs estimating.

## How to use it

<Steps>
<Step index={1} title="Open the scanner">
Pick barcode scanning in the input area. Your browser or the app will ask for
camera permission the first time.
</Step>

<Step index={2} title="Line the barcode up">
Hold steady for a moment. If the camera cannot read it — low light, a creased
label — you can type the digits by hand.
</Step>

<Step index={3} title="Check the product">
Kallo looks the code up in the Open Food Facts database and shows the product
name and its nutrition figures.
</Step>

<Step index={4} title="Choose how to measure it">
Three ways: by the **serving** on the label, by the **whole package**, or by a
**gram amount** you type.
</Step>
</Steps>

## When the product is not found

Open Food Facts is community-maintained, and its coverage of Vietnamese products
is still uneven. If nothing comes back:

- Try [manual logging](/docs/logging/manual) with the main ingredient.
- Or [describe the meal](/docs/logging/describe-a-meal) in words.
- Send us feedback so we know which products are missing.

<Callout label="What the scan sends">
Only the barcode digits are sent to Open Food Facts for the lookup. No image, no
account information. Details in the [privacy policy](/docs/legal/privacy).
</Callout>

## About label accuracy

Package figures are rounded and carry a legally permitted tolerance. They are
usually more accurate than an estimate, but not exact — and the serving on the
label rarely matches what you actually ate. If you had two thirds of the packet,
use the gram amount.
