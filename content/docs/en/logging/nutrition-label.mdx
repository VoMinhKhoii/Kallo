A barcode scan only works when the product is in the database. The nutrition
table printed on the pack always has the numbers, so when the barcode comes up
empty, take a photo of the table and Kallo reads it for you. Label scanning is
part of [Premium](/docs/account/premium).

## How to use it

<Steps>
<Step index={1} title="Open the scanner">
Open scanning in the input area and switch from Barcode to Nutrition label. Take
a photo, pick one from your library, or tap Enter manually to type the values
yourself.
</Step>

<Step index={2} title="Photograph the whole table">
Fit every row of the table in the frame, as flat and as well lit as you can.
Kallo needs the printed table itself, so a photo of the front of the pack, a
menu, a receipt or handwritten notes comes back with a message asking for the
label. If the photo is blurred or cuts off part of the table, retake it.
</Step>

<Step index={3} title="Check what Kallo read">
You see the product name when one is printed near the table, then calories,
protein, carbs and fat, and below them any other nutrients the label lists. Tap
Edit to correct a number. A blank field means the label does not list that
nutrient, and 0 means the food has none of it.
</Step>

<Step index={4} title="Choose how much you had">
Log one serving, the whole pack, or an amount you type in grams, or millilitres
for a drink. Serving appears when the label prints a serving size. Whole pack
appears when the label's numbers are for the whole pack, or when it prints both
a serving size and how many servings the pack holds. Then add the meal.
</Step>
</Steps>

## What Kallo reads from the table

Labels give their numbers per 100 g, per 100 ml, per serving or per pack, and
some print two columns side by side. Kallo notes which one the table uses, so
the amount you pick in step 4 scales from the right base.

Each value is copied with the unit printed next to it. Energy given in kJ is
converted to kcal, and a decimal comma such as 1,5 g reads as one and a half
grams. A nutrient shown only as a percentage of daily value is left blank,
because a percentage is not an amount.

Besides calories, protein, carbs and fat, Kallo reads fiber, sodium, calcium,
iron, magnesium, phosphorus, potassium, zinc, copper, manganese, beta carotene
and vitamins A, B1, B2, B3 (PP), B5, B6, B9, B12, C, D, E, H and K, whenever the
table lists them. Kallo does not track saturated fat, trans fat, cholesterol or
added sugars, so it skips those rows.

<Callout label="What the scan sends">
Only the photo, which goes to Google's Gemini model to read the table. Kallo
asks for your permission for AI analysis before the first scan. Kallo also keeps
the photo privately, without its location or camera details, together with what
was read from it. Only you and the Kallo team can see it. Details in
[your data](/docs/account/your-data) and the [privacy policy](/docs/legal/privacy).
</Callout>

## About label accuracy

The figures on a pack are rounded, and the law allows them some tolerance, so
they are close to the truth but not exact. The serving on the label is also
often not what you ate. If you had half the bag, type the amount instead of
logging a serving.
