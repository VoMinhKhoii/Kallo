How many units — and what happens with four

Two questions, and they turn out to be the same question. The unit count decides which splits are expressible; the number of people decides which splits are needed. Get them to agree and the control works at any size.

The most truthful, and the most expensive. Every person can be set independently, so the meter becomes a read-out rather than a control. Needs per-recipient portions on the wire — four different copy_factor values in one call — which is a materially bigger server change than the one-number version.

The rule that makes both work
1

Units must divide the party. “Even” has to be exactly expressible or the default stops matching today’s behaviour. With P people the meter uses P × m cells, m chosen to land between 4 and 10: two people → 6, three → 6, four → 8, five → 10 (or 5). One cell is always one step.

2

Six is the answer for two people. It is the only small count that expresses a half and a third — ½ ⅓ ⅔ ⅙ — which is how anyone actually describes a shared dish. Four gives you quarters but no “a bit more than half”; ten gives round percentages but no thirds.

3

Five cannot be halved. Straight five units means two people open on 40/60 and can never say 50/50 — the exact split the feature must keep working. Five is right for five people, which is probably the instinct behind the ask: at P=5 the even split IS five units.

4

Print fractions, not percentages. At six units the percentages are 17/33/50/67/83. Write ⅓ and 347 kcal; never “33.3%”. The kcal is the number people can check against their own body, and the fraction is the number they can check against the plate.

5

Only your share is quantised. The remainder splits evenly among the others in kcal, not in whole cells — so N-B works at any party size without the unit count having to divide the remainder too. The hairlines in the tan block are drawn proportionally.

6

N-B costs the same server change as the 1:1 version. One number on the wire (myPortion), one copy_factor shared by every recipient, because the others are equal to each other. N-C is the one that forces per-recipient factors.
