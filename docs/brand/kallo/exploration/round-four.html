Kallo identity study · round four

Built, not
disassembled.

Round-four constructions drop the font's arm geometry entirely. Every option keeps the same skeleton — a 168-wide stem, uniform 56-unit channels, matched stroke weight — but the right side is drawn from pure geometry, not from Avenir's K. Pick a direction; angles and gaps stay tunable after.

Rejected — round one. Arms followed the wordmark's shallow angle and cap/baseline tips, so it read as the font's K taken apart rather than a designed mark.

A · condensed chevron

Steep & upright

Mirrored parallelograms at 30°, condensed into a 453×708 box. The most conservative option — clearly a K, but tighter and more deliberate than the font. Closest to round one.

B · chisel arms

Perfect 45°, mitered tips

Each arm is a true rotated rectangle at exactly 45° — tips cut perpendicular, like a chisel or a folded ribbon. No letterform in any font has this; it reads as a constructed emblem that happens to spell K.

C · parallel ascent

One slab, one cut

The right side is a single 47° slab split by the same vertical 56 channel that separates the stem — every space in the mark is literally identical. The purest geometry and the most abstract; K-ness is at its weakest here, it leans toward a pure "ascent" symbol.

D · carved block

The spaces are the logo

A solid 708×708 square with the K drawn entirely by the 56-unit channels of light — the stem cut and two 45° cuts. Nothing here is a letterform; the K only exists in the negative space. The strongest app-icon silhouette of the four.

All four share the wordmark stem's 168 stroke and the uniform 56 channel, so any of them still locks up with the option B lettering. Choose a direction — or name a hybrid — and the next pass tunes its angle, gap, and proportions only.
